# Tru8 — Buyer Validation Script (2026-06-24)

**Purpose:** before publishing any price, test on real buyers what desk research can't: willingness-to-pay, the £-vs-$ question, whether the no-verdict + provenance positioning lands, and whether there's any API/agent demand in the niche. Target **8–10 conversations.** This is the "validate before you build/publish" step the project keeps relearning.

**What we're testing (the hypotheses):**
1. The "show-your-working" researcher has a real, recurring sourcing-defensibility pain.
2. **No-verdict is a feature, not a dealbreaker,** for them.
3. The signed provenance record + breadth is valued.
4. **£20/mo (fair-use unlimited)** reads as "fair-to-cheap," not expensive.
5. **£** is acceptable (vs $).
6. Whether any of them would want the **API/automation** route.

---

## Who to talk to (UK-first — our source base is UK-heavy)
- Investigative / data journalists; fact-checkers
- Policy & think-tank researchers; parliamentary/public-affairs analysts
- Independent writers/newsletter authors who source seriously (Substack, etc.)
- Research librarians / information professionals
- (Stretch) small-firm litigation/OSINT researchers — higher ACV, different bar

**Where to find them:** Hacks/Hackers chapters, NUJ, journalism Slacks/Discords, Bluesky/X journalism circles, r/journalism, university research/comms depts, policy networks (IfG, RUSI-adjacent), LinkedIn. Warm intros > cold.

---

## Outreach message (short, honest, no pitch)

> Subject: 15 min on how you source-check claims?
>
> Hi [name] — I'm building a research tool that maps the public evidence for and against a claim (across ~30 web/gov/academic sources) and shows your working, deliberately *without* giving a verdict. I'm not selling anything — I'd value 15 minutes on how you currently source-check and defend your work, to make sure I'm building the right thing. Happy to give you free access as a thank-you. Free this week?

---

## Interview script (~15 min; open → specific; price LAST)

Order matters — get their world and their anchor *before* mentioning our price.

1. **Workflow:** "Walk me through how you research and source-check a claim today — what tools, where does it get painful or slow?"
2. **Defensibility:** "When an editor or colleague challenges your sourcing, how do you show your working? Has weak sourcing ever cost you?"
3. **Current spend:** "What do you or your org pay for research / verification / archiving tools now?" *(establishes budget + anchor)*
4. **Reaction (show a real `/r/` report link):** "Here's a check — it maps evidence for and against, classifies every source by type and tier, flags what's missing, and gives a signed record. First reaction?"
5. **No-verdict probe:** "It won't tell you true/false — it shows the evidence, you decide. Feature or dealbreaker?" *(the key hypothesis)*
6. **Provenance probe:** "It produces a signed, shareable record of exactly what was found and what was excluded. Useful — and to whom?"
7. **WTP — let them anchor first:** "If this saved you a couple of hours a week, what would you expect to pay?" *…then test:* "Would **£20/month for fair-use-unlimited** feel cheap, fair, or expensive?"
8. **Currency:** "Does seeing the price in **£ vs $** matter to you at all?"
9. **API / automation:** "Would you — or your team's developers — ever want this via an API or automation, rather than the website? What would you plug it into?"
10. **Buying process:** "Would this be a personal expense or an org purchase? Who signs it off, and how (card / invoice / procurement)?"

Close: "Anything I should have asked? Who else should I talk to?"

---

## Reading the signal (go / hold / rethink)

**GREEN (proceed, publish £20):**
- They name a real, recurring sourcing-defense pain (Q2) AND already pay for an adjacent tool (Q3).
- No-verdict lands as fine/positive (Q5).
- £20 reads "fair" or "cheap" (Q7), £ is a non-issue (Q8).

**YELLOW (adjust):**
- Interested but £20 reads "expensive" → test £12–15, or lead with Teams.
- No-verdict tolerated but they "want an answer too" → positioning/onboarding work, not a kill.
- $ strongly preferred by most → revisit currency.

**RED (rethink before publishing):**
- No-verdict is a repeated dealbreaker (they want the verdict) → the whole wedge is wrong.
- No one pays for adjacent tools / no budget exists → niche won't convert at any price.
- API interest is the *only* warmth → the human-sub thesis is weaker than the agent one.

## Logistics
- 15 min, video or call; record with consent; one shared notes sheet (one row per person, columns = the 10 questions + green/yellow/red).
- Offer **extended free access** as thanks (marginal cost ~2¢/check — trivial).
- Aim for **8–10** before drawing conclusions; stop early only if the first 5 are unanimous.
- Founder runs these (you're the credible founder voice). I can prep the notes sheet, the `/r/` demo links, and synthesise the results into a go/hold/rethink call.
